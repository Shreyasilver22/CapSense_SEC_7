# Differential feedback interface for the 10 kHz SC readout

The existing SC readout drives a second copy of the final telescopic OTA. SC OUTP connects through Rin to the added OTA INP, with Rf from its OUTM to INP. SC OUTN connects through Rin to INN, with Rf from OUTP to INN. All four resistors are equal and the ideal closed-loop differential gain is +1 V/V. The OTA uses 5 V, 3.5 µA IREF, 3.0 V VBN, 2.8 V VBP, a 2.5 V VCM target, and a 5 pF load at each output. The SC keeps 40 pF feedback capacitors and its original clock timing.

## Nominal simulation

The main screening case uses CP/CN = 12/7 pF, 27 °C, typical MOS and RPP, and a sample at 80 µs into the cycle. The two OTAs use the capacitance-extracted macro. The native RPP model sets resistance. A separate engineering sensitivity test adds shunt capacitance split equally between the two terminals of each resistor. The scaled test uses 5.32 pF per 200 kΩ, based on the earlier CMFB resistor body estimate. This approximation does not represent an extracted layout of the four resistors. The original screen also tests half that capacitance.

With four 500 kΩ resistors and 13.3 pF per resistor, the sampled differential voltage is 756.38 mV for the SC alone, 737.36 mV at the loaded SC output and 706.83 mV after the added OTA. The added stage tracks its loaded input with a ratio of 0.959 V/V at this endpoint. The total loss relative to the SC alone is 6.55%. The added OTA output common-mode is 2.4954 V. Its measured settling time to the 88 µs value is about 34.7 µs from TX switching at 46 µs, using a band of 0.1% of the late value or 100 µV, whichever is greater. Only 34 µs is available before sampling. The second-cycle 30 ns and fifth-cycle 10 ns results agree within 3 µV at the sample. At 7/12 pF the added OTA gives −706.06 mV and at 10/9 pF it gives +141.74 mV. The 7/7 pF and 12/12 pF cases give residual differentials of about +0.30 and +0.43 mV.

The 400 kΩ case settles in 24.5 µs under the scaled capacitance assumption but gives only 693.63 mV. The 600 kΩ and larger scaled cases miss the 80 µs settling window. With no added resistor capacitance, 10 MΩ improves the output to 753.62 mV, but adding the scaled body capacitance prevents settling in time. The 500 kΩ case is a bench example near the trade-off, not a qualified unity-gain implementation. Differential tracking error and dynamic settling are separate measurements. These runs do not establish performance with extracted resistor parasitics, mismatch, sampled noise, PVT, or an actual ADC load.

Run ngspice -b unity_500k.cir to reproduce the bench and write the waveform. Run python3 check.py and python3 extend.py for the resistance sweeps, then python3 plot.py for the figure. These scripts reuse run.py from the parent validation directory and need the existing Linux project and SCL08 models.
