# NMOS versus PMOS feedback capacitors

The bench is the same 10 kHz SC readout with the capacitance-extracted telescopic OTA, grounded RX sampling clamp, and 2.5 V TX transfer level. Nominal conditions are typical SCL08 models, 5 V, 27 °C, and sampling at 80 µs in the second cycle. The ideal 40 pF and the 97-unit PMOS bank are controls. All MOS units have W = 20 µm and L = 10 µm.

The test compares both gate orientations with the NMOS body tied to the S/D plate and with the body grounded. The first option assumes an isolated NMOS body that can follow the signal plate. Its physical availability in this PDK has not been established. The second option models a standard grounded NMOS substrate. To measure mutual capacitance the fixture applies AC to the shorted S/D plate and measures gate current with the gate AC-grounded. This matters for the grounded body, because some gate capacitance couples to ground rather than to the feedback terminal. The C–V sweep holds S/D at 2.5 V and varies gate bias at 10 kHz. Initial bank sizes use approximately 40 pF of charge-equivalent capacitance from reset to ±0.96 V on this curve.

At CP/CN = 12/7 pF, ideal feedback gives 505.02 mV and the PMOS bank gives 504.84 mV. The two isolated-body NMOS orientations give 286.68 mV (gate at OUT, 191 units) and 359.33 mV (gate at SUM, 169 units). Their local sensitivities are 55.35 and 64.54 mV/pF, respectively. The C–V curve is steep enough that calibration at a single effective bias is a poor predictor of in-circuit gain. Grounded-body NMOS has about 11 fF of mutual capacitance per unit near the sizing bias. This implies thousands of devices and introduces large parasitic capacitance to ground. At 12/7 pF, the output changes by another 42–43 mV between 80 and 88 µs in the grounded-body variants. The 80 µs slope and sensor-noise propagation in those variants are time-specific results for an unsettled waveform.

Sensor noise propagation uses independent kT/C charge perturbations at the sensor nodes in the clocked circuit. It does not establish total sampled noise. This study uses schematic transistor banks without layout parasitics, mismatch or extracted wells. Run python3 compare.py and python3 plot.py. Testbench paths refer to the installed Linux SCL08 models and final OTA.

A retune of 95, 105, and 115 isolated-body NMOS units per branch with gate at SUM bracketed the target nominal signal. A 100-unit bank then gave 503.86 mV, 94.72 mV/pF, and 7.61 µV RMS sensor-only differential noise, or 80.4 aF referred to capacitance. The PMOS bank gives 504.84 mV, 95.79 mV/pF, and 6.92 µV RMS or 72.3 aF. At 12/12 pF the retuned NMOS input common-mode dips to 0.796 V, versus 1.102 V for PMOS. At the four selected model/supply/temperature combinations, the retuned NMOS differs from ideal feedback by up to 37.49 mV at 12/7 pF. The PMOS differs by up to 40.96 mV in those same cases. These screens do not establish process yield. The retuned NMOS is a useful electrical comparison and still requires an isolated body option and physical validation.
