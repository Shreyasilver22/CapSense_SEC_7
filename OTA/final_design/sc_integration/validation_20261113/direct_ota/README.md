# Direct connection from the 10 kHz SC readout to the telescopic OTA

Connect SC OUTP to OTA INP and SC OUTN to OTA INN. The added OTA uses the final capacitance-extracted macro, 5 V, 3.5 µA IREF, VBN = 3.0 V, VBP = 2.8 V, VCM = 2.5 V, and 5 pF on each output. The output common-mode feedback stays active. The added OTA has open-loop differential inputs, as requested for this direct connection. The upstream test keeps 40 pF and the original 10 kHz timing. It uses nominal SCL08 MOS and RPP models at 27 °C.

With the original SC readout driving a second OTA, at CP/CN = 12/7 pF the fifth-cycle sample has approximately 2.873 V and 2.117 V at the OTA inputs. The OTA outputs are 3.924 V and 0.911 V, for +3.012 V differential and 2.417 V output common-mode. Reversing the capacitor imbalance gives −3.012 V. This is the open-loop large-signal limit. The added OTA draws about 233 µW from its main supply and the upstream readout draws about 242 µW. Ideal bias, clock and reference drivers are excluded.

At 7/7 pF the SC differential output is about +0.325 mV, while the second OTA gives +0.657 V at the fifth sample. At 12/12 pF the SC gives about +0.788 mV and the added OTA gives +1.529 V. At 12/12 pF the added OTA output changes by about 44 mV between the 80 and 88 µs points of the fifth cycle. The sample history records the continuing drift across five cycles.

For the alternative interpretation, removing the OTA inside the SC leaves a passive network whose existing reset shorts each feedback capacitor. With that network directly driving the final OTA at 12/7 pF, the sampled input average is −0.322 V and the differential input is −10.5 mV. Adding a 2.5 V precharge switch per branch during reset gives −0.186 V input average and −93.6 mV differential at the transfer sample. Charge redistribution and switch junction conduction determine the resulting levels. The simulated values are diagnostic because the passive network drives the OTA outside its intended input range. These two connections do not provide a usable linear readout.

The figure compares the three wiring options for the same 12/7 pF case. The first-cycle files use a 50 ns maximum step, as do the KLU fifth-cycle cross-checks. The endpoint CSV also includes results at 20 ns with the default solver. The provided active_sc_12_7.cir can be run in ngspice on the Linux system with the final project and SCL08 models installed. Model translation warnings and capacitance extraction limitations carry over. These tests use ideal external sources and nominal process conditions. No ADC input circuit is attached.

A centered ±0.05 pF perturbation of CP about 12 pF with CN fixed at 7 pF gives 140.32 mV/pF at the SC outputs and 101.71 mV/pF at the added OTA outputs. The local gain is 0.725 V/V in this compressed region even though the ratio of the endpoint voltages is 3.98 V/V. The two figures differ because the added OTA is operated open loop. Its CMFB controls the output average and leaves the differential gain open loop.

The sensor-only first-cycle calculation perturbs the charge on each sensor capacitor and propagates independent kT/C variance to the 80 µs sample. See active_sensor_noise_first_cycle.csv. This estimates the transfer of sensor thermal noise around the compressed 12/7 pF operating point. A total sampled noise or resolution specification would require switch, reset, OTA and clocked noise folding analysis in a valid linear operating range.

The completed direct-connection comparisons cover four capacitance endpoints and the local sensitivity points listed above. The sensor-only result is 6.90 µV RMS differential at the added OTA inputs and 5.00 µV RMS at its outputs. This reduction tracks the compressed local gain. The original SC readout remains the upstream source in the active test. The existing clock and transfer switches remain part of that readout.

Run ngspice -b active_sc_12_7.cir from this folder for the principal case. Run python3 simulate.py and python3 plot.py to regenerate the endpoint comparisons and plots. The scripts reuse the parent validation run.py and the sibling passive_outputs/simulate.py.
